## 1. The service

The service lets you run a free prize draw at an in-person event: people join on their phones with a display
name and an email address, you draw prizes with public randomness, and the draw is published so anyone can
verify it. The service is provided as is, free of charge, and may change or stop. We do our best to keep it
running during events but give no availability guarantee.

## 2. Your responsibilities

- You run free draws only: no purchase, donation or payment to enter. You are responsible for the lawfulness
  of your raffle (prize-promotion, gambling and tax rules) where you run it.
- You name yourself (or your organization) truthfully as the organizer. The privacy notice entrants see names
  you as the data controller, with the contact you provide.
- You honour the erasure dates the service shows your entrants, and the requests they make to you about their
  data. You can always erase earlier; nothing extends a date. You mark prizes delivered as they reach their
  winners, so their data stops being held; we may remind you, and may review what is held past the event.
- Winners' details you export are your copies, outside the service: you keep, protect and erase them under
  your own rules.
- You keep your sign-in credentials safe and tell us about any suspected misuse.

## 3. Acceptable use

No raffles that are unlawful, discriminatory, or that collect data for anything other than running the draw
and delivering prizes. We may suspend or remove a raffle or an account that breaks these terms.

## 4. Data processing agreement (GDPR Article 28)

For the personal data of your raffle's entrants (display names, email addresses, claim codes, entry times and
draw results), **you are the controller and we are your processor.** This section is the data processing
agreement between us.

**Subject matter and duration.** Running your raffles: collecting entries, drawing, publishing the verifiable
draw, and erasing personal data on the dates shown, for as long as you use the service and until the last
erasure date has passed.

**Nature and purpose.** Storing entries, telling them apart, identifying winners, letting you contact winners
to deliver prizes, erasing personal data on schedule.

**Your documented instructions.** We process entrants' data only as the service's documented behaviour
describes (the privacy notice at `/privacy/<raffle>`, the retention settings you choose within the limits we
set, your actions in the admin) and as the law requires. We tell you if we believe an instruction breaks the
law.

**Confidentiality.** People who can access the data are bound to confidentiality.

**Security.** Transport encryption, access control on the admin, no personal data in logs, erasure on
schedule, and the measures our privacy notice describes. We tell you without undue delay about a personal
data breach affecting your raffles.

**Subprocessors.** You authorise the subprocessors our privacy notice at `/privacy` lists (hosting, the raffle
engine, organizer sign-in, the public randomness network). We tell you of changes by updating that notice and
its version; you may object by stopping use of the service.

**Assistance.** The admin gives you the tools to answer entrants' requests: you see their data, can correct a
display name, erase one entrant or everyone, and export winners. We help with what the admin can't do, within
reason.

**End of processing.** When you remove your data from the service ("Remove my data" in the admin, by an
organization admin for an organization), remaining personal data of your raffles is erased and your name and
contacts are taken off them; the anonymous draws stay verifiable. Entrants' data is erased on the dates shown
whether or not you do. Deleting your account with the sign-in provider does not by itself remove your data
here: do that first. We keep the record of your acceptance of these terms.

**Audit.** We make available the information needed to show compliance with this section, and allow audits
you reasonably request, at your cost.

## 5. Our own processing

independent controller; our privacy notice at `/privacy` describes it.
